You have your real estate course open in RELO.

Where do you start, and how will you know your study is working?

At the top: your course, a search for any concept, and your target exam date.

Down the left: Lessons, Content Catalog, Analytics, Review and Support.

Your Study Navigator suggests what to work on next.

It pairs learning with practice.

Watch opens the lesson material for a topic.

Level Up takes you to the activities that build that topic's mastery.

Beside them: lesson progress, and the mastery points left to the next milestone.

Watching a lesson helps you learn.

Answering practice questions is what gives RELO evidence of your understanding.

The Navigator adjusts as you work, so come back whenever you need a next step.

Let's open the lesson it recommends.

The player keeps the topic list beside the video.

When something matters, save a note at that point in the video and come back to it later.

When you are ready to practise, use Level Up.

This percentage is your mastery of the concept.

Under it, the next milestone: ten points to Basic.

Those are mastery points, not a promise that ten questions get you there.

Choose questions and a session length you can finish.

Answer, submit, then spend time with the explanation.

Why the correct answer fits, and why the others do not.

If an idea keeps catching you out, save a short note in your own words.

Finish the set, read the summary, then look at the concept again.

Twenty percent has become twenty-five.

Treat that as feedback about where you are, not a fixed reward for each answer.

One good set does not finish a concept.

Flashcards are there for when you understand an idea but cannot yet recall it.

ReadyRating answers a broader question: how ready are you for this exam?

Expand the card for its one to five scale.

Concept mastery helps you choose work inside a topic.

ReadyRating is your overall readiness signal.

It is not the percentage of lessons watched, and it is not your last quiz score.

Your target exam date puts that work on a timeline.

It plans your study; it does not book the official exam.

Pacing moves through Launch, Study and Final Review.

Forward Pace shows the pace each target needs.

Track Pace compares your real progress against it.

Analytics brings your activity and your results together.

Practice Stats gathers your question activity, scores, flashcards and practice exams.

Readiness Insights suggests a focus.

Mastery Ranges breaks it down by exam domain, with each domain's weight.

Find the area that needs work and take a quiz straight from it.

Below it, Practice Pace Insights compares your progress with the pace your date asks for.

Review is where you go back: recent questions with their explanations, and your saved notes.

Under Practice, read the practice exam instructions before you begin.

Inside, watch the timer, use the question navigation, and flag anything to return to.

Finish Exam asks you to confirm before it grades.

A RELO practice exam is separate from the official licensing exam.

If an explanation still feels unclear, ask Riley a specific question, then a follow up.

Riley helps you understand.

Work there does not count toward mastery, so graded practice is yours alone.

Support is where you reach the team and book office hours.

When you arrange the official exam, follow RELO and RECA's current instructions for authorization and booking.

Nothing here books it for you.

So: learn, practise, review, adjust.

Next session, open your Study Navigator and take the step it gives you.
